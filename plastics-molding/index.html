What each entry gives you

The term engineers actually use

One preferred rendering per language, plus accepted variants and the abbreviation where the industry uses one.

The renderings to avoid

Dictionary calques, false friends and terms for a neighbouring concept, each with a short reason, for 213 terms where the error really occurs. Renderings that give mold opening force for clamping force, or melt volume-flow rate (MVR) for melt mass-flow rate (MFR), are flagged. They carry the TBX deprecated status, which translation tools that read it can show as forbidden terms and flag in your text.

A source and evidence

An English definition written for this termbase, the standard or reference it rests on, and the public documents that show the Japanese translation in use.

How terms are verified

No term is included because it sounds right. Each one passes the same checks, and terms that fail any of them are held back for a later update rather than guessed.

  1. Sourced headwordEvery English headword is tied to an ISO, IEC or JIS standard, an industry glossary, technical literature or a public catalogue.
  2. Blind back-translationEach translation is translated back to English by a separate pass that has not seen the original, and must return the same concept.
  3. Definition matchEach translation is checked against the definition, so a term for a whole machine or mold is not given for a single part, or the reverse.
  4. Public evidenceThe Japanese translation must appear in a national or international standard, in the published documents of at least two unrelated manufacturers, in an industry-association or public-body publication, or in at least three papers across two or more journals on J-STAGE. Renderings to avoid were reviewed a second time, and any that manufacturers or research institutes actually use for the same concept were removed.
