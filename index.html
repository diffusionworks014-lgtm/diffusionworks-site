What each entry gives you

The term engineers actually use

One preferred rendering per language, plus accepted variants and the abbreviation where the industry uses one.

The renderings to avoid

Dictionary calques and false friends, each with a short reason. Translation tools import them as deprecated terms and can flag them in your text.

A source and evidence

An English definition written for this termbase, the standard or reference it rests on, and the public documents that show each translation in use.

How terms are verified

No term is included because it sounds right. Each one passes the same checks, and terms that fail any of them are held back for a later update rather than guessed.

  1. Sourced headwordEvery English headword is tied to a SEMI, ISO, IEC, JIS or JEDEC standard, an industry glossary, or a recognised technical reference.
  2. Blind back-translationEach translation is translated back to English by a separate pass that has not seen the original, and must return the same concept.
  3. Definition matchEach translation is checked against the definition, so a term for the whole tool is not given for a single part, or the reverse.
  4. Public evidenceThe translation must appear in a national standard, in the published documents of at least two unrelated equipment makers, in an industry-body glossary, or across several journals in that language.
